THE CALL

What important IR research problems are still not solved by LLMs?

LLMs have changed what is easy to do in information retrieval. They have not finished the field. We are collecting research directions from the next generation of IR researchers and bringing them together into a research agenda for the field’s future.

We want to hear from the next generation

Much of the research agenda in IR has traditionally been shaped by established researchers. But the researchers who will spend the next five or ten years working on the field’s open problems should have a say in what those problems are.

This is an invitation to put those ideas on the table.

Send us a research direction

What is an important IR problem that remains unsolved? What should researchers be working on next?

Send us a few hundred words describing your idea. It can be a problem, a question, a gap, or a direction you think the field should pursue.

There is no competitive selection process. We want to collect ideas from across the community, bring them together, and develop them into a research agenda.

If you contribute a research direction, you become a co-author of the resulting research agenda paper.

WHAT TO WRITE

Your research direction

Tell us about an IR research problem that you think is still worth pursuing. It can be a problem that:

  • LLMs do not solve
  • LLMs change but do not eliminate
  • requires new methods, systems, evaluations, or user research
  • you think deserves substantial future research

There is no required format. We are interested in the problem itself. You do not need to write a full paper or a position paper.

THE PAPER

A collective research agenda for IR

The submitted research directions are brought together and developed into a research agenda for information retrieval. Ideas are clustered into themes, discussed, and synthesized during the workshop in Berlin.

Contributors are co-authors of the resulting research agenda paper. The aim is a document that identifies the IR research problems worth the next generation's time.

FAQ

Questions

Is this a normal workshop submission?

No. We are asking for short research directions, not full workshop papers.

Will ideas be rejected?

The intention is to include all submitted research directions and to use clustering and discussion to organize and develop them.

How long should my submission be?

A few hundred words is enough.

What do I get by contributing?

You become a co-author of the resulting research agenda paper.

Do I need to attend Berlin?

No. You can contribute without attending the in-person activity.

Does my idea have to be completely new?

No. It needs to identify an important research problem that remains open and worth pursuing.
